# Index and hub editorial mode

Use this mode for an article index, column homepage, knowledge hub, or another multi-entry landing page. The editorial unit is the reader's model of the whole collection, not one paragraph or entry at a time.

A structurally valid list can still fail editorially. Correct headings, working links, and polished entry summaries do not prove that readers understand the collection's system boundary, why its parts belong together, or where to begin.

## Preserve the general contracts

Keep the main Skill's `EDIT`, `EXPAND`, and `AUDIT` boundaries, evidence rules, formal masthead, and release terminal states. A link is not proof that its target is current, published, or read back; carry forward the verified state actually established by the publishing workflow.

Treat a taxonomy, first-screen explanation, entry role, or ordering change as globally consequential even when it changes only a few lines. It may remain a focused edit if the system model and entry map stay valid; otherwise use a structural rewrite. In `AUDIT`, report the global failure without silently repairing it.

## 1. Define the system boundary

Write four working statements before arranging entries:

- **Reader** — who arrives here and which decision or task brings them.
- **System boundary** — what belongs in this collection and what does not.
- **Shared result** — the outcome, capability, or decision all entries jointly support.
- **Relationship model** — causal chain, operating loop, layered architecture, lifecycle, or another sourced relationship that explains the whole.

Use a causal chain only when the source supports causality. If the entries are merely adjacent topics, say so, rename the collection, split it, or leave the relationship `Unknown`; do not invent a loop to make the page feel complete.

## 2. Build one entry map

Inventory the collection before rewriting local copy:

| Entry | Reader question | Unique role | Upstream/downstream relation | Canonical target | State/date |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mainline or article | What brings the reader here | What only this entry contributes | How it connects to the system | Main-title link | Verified state and separate date |

The map should expose duplicates, missing handoffs, inconsistent abstraction levels, competing names, and entries whose descriptions promise more than their targets establish. Categories need not be perfectly mutually exclusive, but every overlap needs an intelligible reason.

Do not let organization names, product surfaces, technical mechanisms, and business outcomes appear as peer categories without explaining the bridge between those levels.

## 3. Satisfy the first-screen contract

Before a change log or full inventory, the first screen must let the intended reader answer:

1. What is this page for?
2. Why these entries belong to one system.
3. Which entry matches my question or desired outcome?
4. What is durable navigation, and what changed recently?

Use the smallest sufficient explanation: often one system thesis plus a short reader-oriented route is enough. Add a diagram only when relationships are genuinely easier to understand visually and every edge is source-supported.

A subtitle that only counts mainlines or repeats the page title does not satisfy this contract. A “start here” list provides entry clarity only when its labels correspond to the system model and the descriptions distinguish the routes.

## 4. Separate durable structure from current updates

Keep the stable system map, canonical entry routes, and current-period changes as distinct layers. Time-ordered updates may help returning readers, but they must not displace the explanation needed by a first-time reader.

Prefer one canonical entry per role. Put alternate versions, superseded drafts, and same-topic evolution in version notes unless they offer a distinct reader job. Never imply that recency makes an entry primary without an explicit editorial reason.

## 5. Enforce entry clarity

Each visible entry needs:

- a link label that matches the target's main title;
- one sentence naming the reader question, judgment, or result it uniquely serves;
- a status and date that are separate from the title when shown;
- terminology consistent with the first-screen model and neighboring entries;
- no unsupported claim that the target is live, complete, current, or canonical.

If two entry summaries are interchangeable, the taxonomy is not finished. If one entry participates in several routes, name its primary role and cross-link the secondary route rather than duplicating it without explanation.

## 6. Run the global coherence pass

Run this global coherence pass after local editing and link validation:

1. **First screen only** — ask a cold reader to state the system boundary, shared result, relationship model, and first useful route.
2. **Headings and entries only** — verify that names, ordering, abstraction level, and status language tell the same story without body prose.
3. **End to end** — verify that every entry fulfills a distinct role, all promised handoffs exist, updates do not contradict durable navigation, and the ending or maintenance note uses the same canonical model.
4. **Target readback** — where publication is in scope, separately confirm each canonical link and its target title/state through the publishing workflow.

Local polish cannot compensate for a failed global model. Do not declare an Index/Hub ready when a reader can describe the topics but cannot explain why they form one system or choose an entry with confidence.

## Readiness decision

- **Ready** — the first-screen contract, entry map, global coherence pass, applicable quality gates, link checks, and requested release verification all pass.
- **Revise** — the collection belongs together, but the system explanation, taxonomy, ordering, or entry clarity needs a bounded repair.
- **Return to source** — the system boundary or relationship cannot be supported, entries conflict materially, or the requested canonical state is Unknown.
